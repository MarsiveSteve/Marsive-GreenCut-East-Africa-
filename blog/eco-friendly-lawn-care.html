Eco-Friendly Lawn Care: Practical Tips for Kenyan Gardens

Smarter mowing, watering and feeding for a healthy lawn that uses less water and fewer chemicals.

Book a Service WhatsApp Us

By Marsive GreenCare East Africa · Lawn care

A green lawn does not have to cost the earth. With a few changes to how you mow, water and feed, you can keep grass healthy while using less water and fewer chemicals. These tips suit lawns across Nairobi and Central Kenya.

1. Mow at the right height

Cutting grass too short stresses it and lets weeds in. As a rule, never remove more than about a third of the blade in a single mowing. Slightly taller grass shades the soil, holds moisture better and develops deeper roots.

2. Leave the clippings

If the clippings are short and dry, leave them on the lawn. They break down quickly and return nutrients to the soil. If the grass has grown long, collect the clippings and add them to a compost heap instead.

3. Water deeply and early

Water thoroughly but less often, and do it early in the morning when it is cooler and less water evaporates. Frequent shallow watering keeps roots near the surface, which makes the lawn weaker in dry weather. A well-designed irrigation system takes the guesswork out of it.

4. Feed the soil, not just the grass

Healthy soil grows healthy grass. Compost and other organic matter improve soil structure and help it hold water. Before adding any fertiliser, think about what the lawn actually needs, and avoid applying more than necessary, especially before heavy rain.

5. Deal with weeds early and gently

A thick, healthy lawn crowds out most weeds. Pull weeds by hand while they are small and patch bare areas quickly so weeds cannot move in.

6. Match the grass to the place

Kikuyu grass is widely used in Kenyan highland gardens and copes well with foot traffic. Warmer areas may suit other grasses such as Bermuda grass. Choosing the right grass for your altitude and use saves effort and water for years.

7. Keep equipment in good shape

Sharp mower blades cut cleanly and heal faster than blades that tear the grass. Regular servicing also keeps equipment running efficiently.
